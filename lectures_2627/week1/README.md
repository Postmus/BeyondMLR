# Beyond MLR: Week 1

**Randomized block designs** — *Modeling center effects and comparing treatments*

Slides 2–5 introduce the course: why grouping matters for iid error assumptions, the before/during/after-class workflow, Quarto reports and submission of both source and HTML with code visible, and certificate/assessment requirements. The 80% attendance requirement, weekly assignment submissions and final-assignment grading are confirmed unchanged for this edition. The course introduction leads directly into Part 1.

The statistical content starts with a multicenter rehabilitation trial and asks whether an initial treatment-only model adequately represents the study:

1. The opening introduces all 480 patients in six centers, with 40 patients randomized to each program within every center. Slide 7 names this a randomized block design and identifies centers as the blocks. Outcomes are first pooled by treatment. Slide 9 introduces the systematic part as the model-predicted outcome, interprets the coefficients, and defines the error as the difference between the observed outcome and that prediction. It leaves the error assumptions to the following slides. Slide 10 introduces zero expected error and recaps expected value, variance and covariance in a table linked to the errors. Slide 11 introduces independence and explains that it implies zero covariance, contrasting independently generated errors with dependent errors showing positive covariance across hypothetical repetitions. The panels have matching means, variances and scales. Slide 12 repeats the treatment-only equation and completes it with the iid Normal error specification. It explains the distribution symbol, zero mean and common variance, and independent and identically distributed errors alongside the outcome-distribution figure. The initial results follow on slide 13: 23.6 m (SE 2.76 m; 95% CI 18.2–29.1 m). Slide 14 places the two diagnostic plots above explanations of their purpose and concludes that they do not suggest clear violations of Normality or equal variance. The broader discussion of model adequacy begins on the following slides.
2. Slide 15 asks whether the treatment-only model reflects the study design. A means-only plot shows observed standard-care, enhanced-care and overall means for each of the six centers, without individual patient observations. Centers are on the vertical axis and mean walking distance on the horizontal axis; all three means align on the same row for each center, with no jitter or offsets. The slide focuses on possible center differences in expected outcomes, before introducing fixed or random center effects. Slide 16 compares a model accounting for treatment and center with the treatment-only model. The former specifies iid Normal remaining errors with mean zero and common variance; the latter places the center difference in its error. The closing question asks whether, if the first model is correct, the treatment-only errors still satisfy the original iid zero-mean Normal assumption. This is a conceptual example, separate from the case-study EDA. Part 1 ends on slide 17 with a comparison of fixed and random center effects: resampling patients in the same centers versus sampling centers and patients. It introduces separate center parameters versus a distribution of center effects, then leads into the fixed-center perspective.
3. Part 2, “Modeling center as a fixed effect” (slides 18–30), begins with a general introduction to simulation on slide 19: specify a known model, generate repeated samples, and examine the consequences of leaving something out. It introduces model misspecification without returning to the six-center case study. Slide 20 specifies the two-center data-generating model and its expected outcomes, without specifying a sample size. Slide 21 draws four patients, one per treatment in each center, and shows how each drawn individual error is added to the expected outcome. Slide 22 uses that same sample to compute the treatment-only errors when center is omitted. The center effects remain fixed when sampling new patients each repetition. The simulation sets the baseline to 100, treatment effect to 5, center contributions to −10 and +10, and independent Normal individual errors with mean 0 and SD 5. It uses the known treatment-only component to compute errors. One sampled study is followed by four histograms at 20 and 10,000 repetitions, using cumulative draws, identical axes and identical bins. Advance the slides to reveal the developing distributions. The next slide pairs errors from the same repetitions: u₁₁ against u₂₁ within center 1, and u₁₁ against u₁₂ across centers. Both pairs have model covariance zero; dashed lines mark their respective expected errors. Conclusion boxes on slides 24 and 25 highlight the equal-variance Normal error distributions with center-specific means, and the independence and zero covariance within and across centers. Slide 26 brings these findings together in the assumption review. Each position has expected error −10 or +10, despite a pooled expected error of 0. After the four-assumption review, slide 27 introduces bias and precision using four schematic dartboards: rows compare no bias with bias, and columns compare high with low precision. Each dart represents a treatment estimate from one simulated study, and the bullseye represents the true treatment effect. Equal-weight definitions accompany the figure, with precision described as the spread of estimates across repeated studies, measured by the standard error. Slide 28 presents two consequences side by side: the treatment estimate is unbiased, with E[β̂₁] = β₁, and its usual standard error is too large on average. Each consequence is followed by a short explanation connecting it to balanced treatment allocation within centers. Slide 29 is a simple “Time for Lab 1” break slide with a generated illustration and no questions or tasks. It replaces the center-adjustment explanation. An initial Lab 1 draft is available in `../../labs_2627/BLR_lab_center_fixed.qmd`. The former center-indicator and interpretation slides have been removed; slide 30 is the Lab 1 debrief, comparing the treatment estimates, standard errors, confidence intervals and residual SDs from both models. The section ends with the Lab 1 debrief.
4. Part 3, “Modeling center as a random effect” (slides 31 onward), opens with the fixed-versus-random bridge on slide 32. Slides 33–40 mirror the Part 2 simulation: specify the generating model, draw two center effects and four patients, calculate treatment-only errors, repeat for 20 and 10,000 studies, examine paired errors, review assumptions, and discuss bias and precision. Center effects are iid Normal with mean 0 and SD 10; individual errors remain iid Normal with mean 0 and SD 5, independent of center effects. The baseline is 100 and treatment effect 5. Each repetition draws new center effects and patients, with one patient per treatment in each center. Marginal treatment-only errors are Normal with mean 0 and variance 125; within-center covariance is 100 and between-center covariance zero. Balanced allocation cancels the center contributions in the treatment estimate: theoretical mean 5 and sampling variance 25. The generator checks these identities and moments, including expected squared treatment-only SE 125, and cross-checks its calculations against OLS fits. Slide 40 repeats the same consequence columns and equation as slide 28. Its closing box states: “Different error structure, same consequences: fixed center effects give center-specific error means; random center effects create dependence within centers.” Slide 41 is a simple “Time for Lab 2” break with a generated illustration. The lab will introduce fitting the random-intercept model, extracting the treatment contrast and SE, and interpreting variance components and ICC. Slide 42 debriefs the three-model treatment comparison, and slide 43 debriefs variance components and both interpretations of ICC. These replace the former trial recap, model-introduction, comparison and simulator-discussion sequence. Slide 44 concludes with “Let the analysis reflect the design”: four points connect balance within groups, appropriate uncertainty estimates, type I error and power. A comparison table links center blocking in the rehabilitation trial, donor blocking in the laboratory assignment, and stratified clinical trials using factors such as disease severity or sex. The lecture closes with the section-style “The End / Questions” slide (45). An initial Lab 2 draft is available in `../../labs_2627/BLR_lab_center_random.qmd`; a new assignment draft is available in `../../labs_2627/assignments/week1/assignment.qmd`.

The multicenter model assumes a common treatment effect and uses a random center intercept. Each new model has a plain-language interpretation alongside its equation. The proposed Week 2 sequence starts with treatment-by-center interactions and random treatment slopes, followed by cluster randomization and crossed random effects; see the [Week 2 planning notes](../week2/PLANNING.md). These extensions are outside the scope of Week 1. Initial drafts of both labs now follow this lecture; see the [lab development notes](LABS.md). The new [Week 1 assignment](../../labs_2627/assignments/week1/assignment.qmd) applies the same models to a tissue-donor experiment; its initial draft is ready for review.

The presentation uses simulated knee-rehabilitation data from six centers with balanced treatment allocation. These are teaching data and do not represent a real clinical study. Center labels are deliberately permuted: the original A–F cohorts are now D, A, F, C, B and E. Center contributions in alphabetical A–F order are −17, +16, +8, −29, +27 and −5 m. This relabelling preserves all patient outcomes, treatment allocations and treatment-comparison results. The legacy single-center assets retain the original cohort, now Center D.

## Source structure

- `build_week1.js`: PptxGenJS content and slide order.

- `error_interlude.js`: the omitted-center slide and fixed-versus-random sampling perspective, plus retained source for the former expected-error and covariance comparison.
- `fixed_center.js`: the fixed-center section, Lab 1 results debrief and conceptual bridge to random center effects.
- `fixed_center_sampling.js`: general simulation introduction, fixed-center generating model, one sampled study, treatment-only errors, cumulative histograms, assumption review, consequences of omitting center and the Lab 1 break slide.
- `design_conclusion.js`: concluding design principle, inference consequences and links to other study settings.
- `omitted_center_consequences.js`: shared consequence columns and equation for slides 28 and 40.
- `generate_bias_precision.js`: reproducible SVG/PNG dartboard figure, using shared course colors and fonts. Run `npm run bias-precision` to regenerate it.
- `lab2_debrief.js`: Lab 2 break, three-model treatment comparison, and variance/ICC debrief.
- `assets/lab2-break.png`: generated Lab 2 illustration; prompt saved in `assets/lab2-break-prompt.txt`.
- `random_center_sampling.js`: Part 3 random-center simulation slides and assumption/consequence review.
- `generate_random_center_sampling.R`: reproducible random-center simulation, cumulative error histograms, paired-error plots and statistical validation. Run `npm run random-center-simulation` to regenerate it.
- `assets/random-center-sampling.json`: parameters, first study, empirical moments and treatment-estimate checks.
- `generate_fixed_center_sampling.R`: reproducible four-position teaching simulation, histograms and paired-error scatterplots, independent of the rehabilitation dataset.
- `assets/fixed-center-sampling.json`: simulation settings, first study, empirical moments and correlations.
- `generate_interlude_figure.R`: schematic covariance figures; no case-study data are used.
- `generate_interlude_equations.js`: optional regeneration of missing interlude equation assets.
- `generate_data.R`: simulation, figures and model calculations.
- `generate_center_summary.R`: base-R generation of the observed center-means plot from the existing trial data, with checks on allocation and mean calculations. Run `npm run center-summary` to regenerate it independently.
- `assets/center-summary.png`: means-only center plot used on the study-design transition slide.
- `generate_initial_analysis.R`: pooled outcomes, treatment-only diagnostics and initial-model results from the existing complete dataset.
- `initial_model.js`: opening model equations, using `u` for the treatment-only error consistently with the interlude.
- `assets/initial-analysis-results.json`: coefficient estimates, uncertainty and residual variation for the opening.
- `assets/rehabilitation_data.csv`: simulated patient-level dataset.
- `assets/model_results.json`: statistics read by the deck builder.

- `assets/equations/`: tracked high-resolution PNG fallbacks. Generated `.tex` and SVG intermediates remain local and are ignored by Git; equation definitions live in the JavaScript sources.
- `pptxgenjs_helpers/`: local text-sizing, image, LaTeX and QA helpers.
- `../../slides-common/js/`: shared course theme, layout primitives and course builders.
- `../../slides-common/R/common_fig.R`: shared figure palette and sizing conventions.

The lecture focuses on statistical concepts and interpretation; R code and software instructions are reserved for the labs.

Slide text, tables and layout elements are editable. Statistical figures and equations are embedded as images. Equations are authored as LaTeX; PNG fallbacks provide consistent PowerPoint/LibreOffice rendering.

## Rebuild

Run from this directory after installing the Node dependencies in `package.json`. R requires `ggplot2`, `nlme` and `jsonlite`.

```sh
npm install
npm run build
```

Figure generation finishes before the presentation is built. The fixed seed makes the data and model results reproducible. The supplied equation PNGs allow the presentation to render without a local TeX installation.

To regenerate only the fixed-center teaching simulation, run `npm run fixed-center-simulation`. Its checks verify the error means, variances and pairwise correlations against the generating process. Both histogram slides and the paired-error scatterplots use the same simulation draws.

To regenerate only the initial-analysis figures and statistics, run `npm run initial-analysis`. This reads the existing trial data without regenerating it or changing later-slide assets.

To regenerate missing opening and interlude equation assets, run `npm run interlude-equations` with `pdflatex` and `pdftoppm` available. Existing equation PNGs are preserved. The interlude uses the shared `slides-common/js/concept_panels.js` layout helper; other slide builders are unaffected.

The simulator demonstration uses `#/concepts/randomized-block-design` in the separate sampling-simulator project. It compares ordinary regression with a random-intercept model. The presentation's fixed-center comparison is calculated from the lecture dataset.
